# Planning: SignalDesk

## Prospecting use cases

1. **Prioritize the next research task.** Real teams do not have time to pursue every lead equally. Salesforce describes lead scoring as ranking candidates using company, contact and behavioral factors, urges calibrating the criteria against past conversion data, and notes that stale or poor-quality CRM data makes scores less useful ([Salesforce, “What Is Lead Scoring?”](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/lead-scoring/)). A second Salesforce prospecting guide emphasizes qualifying fit as the rep advances the conversation and asking for the appropriate contact, rather than over-investing in a poor fit ([Salesforce, “What Is Sales Prospecting?”](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/prospecting/)). The prototype therefore shows a sort/filter queue, score components, a human disposition, and a contextual next step—not an automated prospect campaign.
2. **Separate possible account evidence from provider inventory.** The supplied JSONL-style service observations include dates, hostnames/domains, provider organization, observed location, ports, TLS/certificate metadata and source vulnerability annotations. They do not include customer identity, firmographics, company size, sector, buyer/contact, technographic budget, prior conversion or engagement. Domain candidates are therefore explicitly unverified. Provider, CDN/cloud and ambiguous multi-domain groups default to **Hold**.
3. **Treat security clues as research, not sales intent.** Database/remote-access ports, a positive legacy-TLS indication, certificate-expired metadata, and CVE flags are separate inspectable clues. A public port does not prove unauthenticated access; source CVE metadata is not independent validation; a cybersecurity clue does not show that a company wants to buy. Freshness applies per signal, so a current benign web service cannot make an old database clue current.
4. **Give a usable local workbench.** Search/filter by candidate, observed country and service segment; review the evidence and attribution caveat; shortlist/export; save dispositions/notes locally; and create an optional reviewed briefing. Observed infrastructure country is not company headquarters or territory jurisdiction.

## Score policy

Deterministic and transparent; not a conversion probability. Score maximum 100 = a 20-point possible single-candidate fit proxy, maximum 60 points for distinct evidence classes that are individually within 30 days (weights 35 verified source CVE / 30 selected data-service ports / 25 remote-access ports / 15 legacy TLS / 15 expired certificate / 5 unverified source CVE), and up to 20 freshness points for a latest observation within seven or 30 days. Multiple records of the same clue do not stack. The review queue needs at least one individually high-weight (25+) positive signal, an eligible candidate and fresh evidence. Weak clues cannot add up to urgent review. This proxy encodes an explainable research order only; no historical sales outcomes are available to fit or validate a statistical ICP.

## Sampling and known limits

The referenced Backblaze object is an **11.06 GiB**, `application/zstd` snapshot dated October 8, 2026, rather than a pre-joined company dataset. The checked-in prototype has a capped sequential prefix of 12,000 records (12,189,696 compressed bytes / 2.56 MB after privacy-conscious allowlisting), not a random or representative sample. Uncompressed records and prefixes may cluster by source timing; counts must not be extrapolated. Reimport with `python scripts\import_dataset.py --records 12000 --max-mb 32`. The importer rejects arbitrary port/domain values, caps each decompressed record, and discards IPs, banners, full certificates and raw source annotations after deriving coarse signals.

Only 1,124 of 1,760 current distinct account/provider groups in this prefix are single non-provider domain candidates; this is not 1,124 verified companies or qualified leads. Four candidate groups initially meet “Review now” after obvious hosting/reverse-DNS exclusions; humans should still enrich ownership. Domain suppression is a small explicit list and is not a reliable hosting-provider/brand classifier. No personal data, names, emails, or contact enrichment are supplied or fabricated.

## Explicit non-goals

No network probing, vulnerability verification, enrichment scraping, account-identity guessing, automatic email sending, CRM integration, funnel probability, customer-list upload, or automatic discovery of named decision-makers. The selected host is now PythonAnywhere with WSGI and persistent account-home SQLite. Deployment scaffolding is prepared, but an actual hosted URL and independent human evaluation remain pending.
